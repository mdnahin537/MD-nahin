### Capacity rule (applies to every model)
Two people at 8 h/week ≈ 70 h/month (ESTIMATE). Founder-delivered work caps total revenue near $1.7–2.8k/month (ESTIMATE at $25–40/h effective). Each model below names its **cap breaker**: AI doing most of the volume, a middleman doing the selling, or other people delivering under our checks.

## M1 Proof-First Fixer
*(was M1 Problem Spotter; absorbs M6 Watchtower as the repeat stage and M12 Benchmark as the scale mode for Read)*

- **What we sell:** a one-page proof, checkable in 30 seconds, that something public is costing money (wrong hours, dead booking link, a bot giving wrong prices, a broken menu feed). Then the fix. Then "kept fixed" monthly checks.
- **Who pays:** first choice, **whoever owns the fix for many businesses**: the agency, ordering-platform reseller or listings manager whose name is on the broken asset. Second, multi-location owners. Single owners serve only as the proving ground ($100–250/month ceiling, per the brief's lessons).
- **How we find and reach them:** audit a niche and city from the outside. For each failure, record the maintainer ("website by X" footer, platform badge, agency portfolio). Group failures by maintainer and send the grouped proof to the maintainer's sales or partnership inbox. **Scale mode:** publish an aggregate benchmark (illustrative wording, no data collected yet: "We checked 120 halal restaurants in East London; N% show wrong Friday hours"). Named businesses and their vendors write to us.
- **First step from home (Monday):**
  1. Pick one niche and city, and write 5 yes/no checks.
  2. Check 20 businesses by hand. AI drafts the comparison; a human confirms every failure with a screenshot.
  3. Note the maintainer for each failure, then write one grouped proof for the maintainer with the most broken clients.
- **Money path:** $150–300 to fix one maintainer's broken clients → $50–100 per client per month "kept fixed", resold by the maintainer under its own name → a maintainer with 20 clients is $1,000–2,000/month (ESTIMATE). Cap breaker: the maintainer sells, and AI runs the checks.
- **Path steps by design:**
  - Strong: Home (no login, about 2 h), Letter (their own failure), Impressed (checkable in 30 s), competence Trust (proof shown first), risk Trust (pay after the fix).
  - Weak: Read. A stranger's proof to an owner lands in message requests or spam. The maintainer route and the benchmark raise it, but it stays the model's weakest step: **flagged**. Pay is medium (Payoneer invoice, card payers lose 3%; SEARCH-SUMMARY, Payoneer fee table).
- **AI and human:** AI runs the checks, drafts the proof and the fix. The human verifies every claim before sending (rule: zero false positives) and signs the report.
- **Why now:** AI answers draw on listing data. AI chat and voice bots now quote prices and hours, so wrong public data costs more than before (ESTIMATE). Bot-liability precedents: Moffatt v. Air Canada 2024; a German appellate court in May 2026 (notes §8, SEARCH-SUMMARY).
- **Hunting grounds (Phase 2):**
  1. *Listing and site maintainers for restaurants and trades* — one maintainer has many broken clients; grouped proof multiplies one letter.
  2. *Online-ordering platform resellers* — their merchant pages are public, and mismatches cost them orders and merchants.
  3. *Multi-location brands (10–200 sites)* — inconsistency scales with locations, and one manager pays for all.
  4. *Agencies whose public AI chat/voice bots give wrong facts* — the proof is the bot's own words (bridge to M4).
  5. *City/niche benchmarks* — a public scoreboard is the letter; test it on one niche first.
  6. *Shothik (NY Bangladeshi restaurants)* — the calibration case (see method.md).
- **Planes to watch:**
  - The fix needs logins. This is the reason to sell to the maintainer, who already has them.
  - One false positive kills credibility.
  - Benchmark naming carries defamation and fairness risk: publish only aggregates and checkable facts, and offer a right of reply before publishing.
  - Automated scraping may break site ToS: check by hand, or use APIs.

## M2 Replacement Desk
*(CEO model, reshaped. Mode B is the new "Verified Talent Bridge" model, merged in.)*

- **What we sell:** a recurring role the client is trying to fill, delivered as a managed service.
  - **Mode A, "we run it":** AI does the volume and a named human owns quality and the error log.
  - **Mode B, "we place and stand behind a verified person":** a Dhaka professional whom we met in person, ID-checked against originals, credential-checked at source and trial-tested. Paid as a flat placement fee or a monthly management fee, with a replacement promise.
- **Who pays:** firms with a **posted, recurring remote role that accepts contractors abroad**: small agencies, e-commerce brands, AI startups needing human-in-the-loop reviewers, professional firms.
- **How we find and reach them:** posted roles where the poster expects outside applicants (Upwork long-term and hourly posts, remote-worldwide boards, contract and "fractional" listings, community hiring threads). Reply fast: within 1–4 hours of the post on Upwork (method.md, tactic T2).
  - Mode A reply: a sample of *their* task made from public material.
  - Mode B reply: two verified profiles, each with a short video of the candidate doing the client's test task.
- **First step from home (Monday):**
  1. Pick one role family whose inputs are public: AI-output review, listing QA, research ops (avoid killed categories).
  2. Collect 30 live posts in 48 h and pick the 5 where a sample can be made from public material.
  3. Mode B: list 10 candidates for that role from the founders' own networks and university groups, then run the verification checklist on 2.
- **Money path:**
  - Mode A: first paid batch $100–300 → $500–1,500/month per client. Capacity caps it at about 2 clients (ESTIMATE).
  - Mode B: flat placement fee $500–1,500, or a $150–400/month margin per placed person (ESTIMATE; flat fees suit small firms better than the 15–25%-of-salary recruiter norm, itself an ESTIMATE). Five placed people make $750–2,000/month recurring without founder hours, so **Mode B is the cap breaker.**
- **Path steps by design:**
  - Strong: Road (posts are public and plentiful), Letter and Reply (they need it now), Read on Upwork (the poster reviews proposals), Repeat (the role recurs).
  - Strong in Mode B: identity Trust by mechanism. The 2025–26 fake-candidate wave is real: Gartner (report dated July 31, 2025) projects 1 in 4 candidate profiles worldwide could be fake by 2028, and 6% of 3,000 surveyed job seekers admitted interview fraud (VERIFIED by two secondary reports, HR Dive and Personnel Today; Gartner release not opened). Google, Cisco and McKinsey reinstated in-person rounds in 2025 (SEARCH-SUMMARY, WSJ via secondary). "We met them in person in Dhaka" answers exactly that fear.
  - Weak: Read off-platform (vendor pitches into hiring funnels are filtered; ESTIMATE). Mode A identity Trust.
- **AI and human:** Mode A: AI does the volume and the human does QA and owns errors. Mode B: AI screens CVs, designs test tasks and summarises interviews. Humans meet candidates, check originals and sign the verification.
- **Hunting grounds (Phase 2):**
  1. *AI startups hiring human-in-the-loop, eval or ops reviewers* — new roles (parity) whose need grows with AI deployment.
  2. *Agencies hiring junior remote delivery roles* — they already buy offshore, and the role recurs.
  3. *Firms publicly hit by fake-candidate or proxy-interview fraud* — Mode B's in-person verification is the direct answer.
  4. *Upwork long-term hourly posts in role families where Bangladesh's bench is deep* (design, development support, research ops) — the platform holds trust and pay.
  5. *E-commerce brands hiring catalogue QA or compliance roles* (not uploads; that is killed) — public-input tasks, so a free sample is possible.
- **Planes to watch:**
  - Many posts are "US/EU only".
  - Mode A hits the 70 h cap fast.
  - Mode B is people management: no-shows, poaching (use a buyout clause), and every bad hire costs our reputation.
  - Keep money flows clean: the client pays the talent directly (Payoneer, Upwork or their contractor tool) and pays our fee separately, so we never run payroll.
  - Bangladesh's overseas-employment recruiting licence probably covers sending workers abroad, not home-based remote work. UNVERIFIED; check before the first placement.
  - The Upwork non-circumvention rule: a conversion fee applies to taking an Upwork client off-platform within 24 months (SEARCH-SUMMARY, Upwork support page and community; exact fee unclear).
  - **Counter-evidence found:** firms are moving final interview rounds in person (SEARCH-SUMMARY). That can disadvantage any offshore candidate. Mode B must offer an in-person proxy (we meet them for you, on video, with originals) or it loses to local hires.

## M3 Revenue Partner
*(was M3 Idea Partner; reshaped so that Pay is visible and Trust is risk-free)*

- **What we sell:** a specific, evidence-backed new revenue line for one owner, opened by a one-page memo and **paid by commission on revenue we can see**: sponsor contracts we bring, tracked codes or links, invoices raised in our name. Optional small setup fee. It doubles as the stage-2 upsell for every other model's clients.
- **Who pays:** owners of an under-monetised asset who **already pay commission sellers**: podcasts and newsletters (sponsorship sales), creators, small brands entering a new channel or market, and SaaS tools with partner programmes.
- **How we find and reach them:** the owner has posted the need. Commission-only ad-sales rep posts exist. Examples found: a freelance podcast sponsorship rep at "straight 20%", and KSL Podcasts at 30% of net sales (SEARCH-SUMMARY, podnews.net and cazvid listings; one marked unverified by the host). "Advertise with us" pages and media kits announce unsold inventory.
- **First step from home (Monday):**
  1. List 10 shows or newsletters that have a commission-rep post or an "advertise" page with empty slots.
  2. For one, build a sponsor-fit memo: 15 brands that already sponsor similar shows, found from public ad reads, with haram categories excluded.
  3. Draft the offer: "commission only for 90 days, you approve every deal".
- **Money path:** the first commission is 20–30% of a $500–2,000 sponsorship, or $100–600 → 2–3 shows with renewing sponsors give $1,000+/month (ESTIMATE). Cap breaker: commission scales with deal size, not hours.
- **Path steps by design:**
  - Strong: Read (they posted the role or the inventory), Reply (zero-risk offer), risk Trust (paid only on results, a halal reward for a result), Repeat (sponsors renew).
  - Weak: Pay. The owner must pay us after the sponsor pays them; collection risk. Mitigation: a written agreement, payment within 7 days of the sponsor paying, and invoicing via Payoneer.
  - Weak: the sponsor side of the sale, which is cold outreach to brand marketers. It is raised by targeting brands that *already buy* similar placements.
- **AI and human:** AI maps who sponsors what from public ad reads and episode notes, drafts pitches and tracks the pipeline. The human handles relationships, negotiation, calls and the halal screen.
- **Why now:** US podcast advertising was about $2.86B in 2025, up 17.6% (IAB/PwC full-year report, April 2026; SEARCH-SUMMARY via trade press), and IAB forecasts about +8.7% in 2026 (SEARCH-SUMMARY). AI makes the sponsor-fit research a medium-sized job instead of a large one (ESTIMATE).
- **Hunting grounds (Phase 2):**
  1. *Podcasts and networks posting commission-only ad-sales roles* — the need is posted and the price (20–30%) is public.
  2. *Niche B2B newsletters with visible unsold sponsor slots* — inventory they want filled, and B2B sponsors avoid most haram categories.
  3. *Muslim-audience creators and media* — halal-clean sponsor fit plus affinity (links to M8).
  4. *Small brands opening a new market or channel* (e.g., UK brand → Gulf/Malaysia) — commission on tracked new-channel sales only.
  5. *SaaS partner programmes we also implement* — recurring referral share stacked on a setup fee (links to M4/M7).
- **Planes to watch:**
  - Haram sponsor categories (betting, alcohol, interest lenders, adult) are common in podcast advertising (ESTIMATE). Screen every deal.
  - Sales cycles of 4–8 weeks before the first dollar (ESTIMATE).
  - US calls fall at Dhaka night.
  - Never hold the client's money; invoice our commission separately.

## M4 New-Category Bench
*(was M4 White-label Bench; absorbs M9 Human Sign-off Desk)*

- **What we sell:** white-label delivery and **named human sign-off** for a service under ~18 months old that agencies already sell but cannot yet deliver or test well. **First product:** pre-launch and monthly QA of AI chat and voice agents that agencies build for small-business clients. We run scripted tests against the client's real facts (prices, hours, policies, escalation), write a red-flag report and fix list, then re-test.
- **Who pays:** small AI-automation and voice-AI agencies, and resellers of white-label voice/chat platforms. Vendor content pushes these reseller programmes hard (Retell, HighLevel, Synthflow, Autocalls and others; SEARCH-SUMMARY, vendor pages). A vendor press release pitches "10 clients × $500/month" (VENDOR CLAIM). Later: the platforms' partner programmes.
- **How we find and reach them:** agencies that publish client bots or demo numbers on case-study pages; platform partner listings (Retell lists partners, including a QA partner since November 2024; SEARCH-SUMMARY); agency communities; posted "test my agent" jobs. The letter is a test report on *their own public demo bot*, sent to the agency, never to its end client.
- **First step from home (Monday):**
  1. Find 10 agencies with a public demo bot (web widget; avoid paid phone calls).
  2. Write 10 test prompts from the demo business's public facts and run them on 2 bots.
  3. Write a one-page signed report: what was wrong, the risk and the fix, and send it to 1 agency.
- **Money path:** $150–300 per bot for pre-launch QA → $75–150 per bot per month for re-tests after every prompt or price change → an agency with 10 bots is $750–1,500/month (ESTIMATE). Cap breaker: the agency sells, and AI runs the test scripts.
- **Path steps by design:**
  - Strong: Letter and Impressed (their bot's own wrong answers), Reply (churn fear and bot-liability precedents: Air Canada 2024, German appellate court May 2026; notes §8), competence Trust (shown, not claimed), parity (nobody has a long track record in agent QA), Pay (B2B, and agencies of this kind already pay offshore contractors; ESTIMATE), Repeat (every change needs a re-test).
  - Weak: Read. Agency founders are reachable but flooded with pitches. **Flagged as medium.**
- **AI and human:** AI writes test scripts, runs chat tests and grades transcripts. The human designs edge cases (the costly ones), confirms every failure and signs. The signature is the product.
- **Why now:** Retell AI ≈ $60M annualised revenue in April 2026, +650% YoY (SEARCH-SUMMARY, Sacra estimate). AI-receptionist adoption is fastest among 1–50-employee firms (VENDOR CLAIM).
- **Hunting grounds (Phase 2):**
  1. *Voice-AI receptionist agencies reselling white-label platforms* — many new agencies, a real bot-error cost, and no QA budget for enterprise tools.
  2. *Chatbot agencies serving clinics, law firms and property managers* — a wrong answer costs the most there.
  3. *Agencies building OpenClaw/Claude agent skills for clients* — ClawHub lists 13k+ skills with no built-in security scanning, per third-party listings (SEARCH-SUMMARY). This is the CEO's own ecosystem.
  4. *Multilingual bots for South Asian diaspora markets* — Bangla, Hindi and Urdu test coverage is our native edge (links to M8).
  5. *Any other category under 18 months old that agencies sell and cannot deliver* — Phase 2 must date each one.
- **Planes to watch:**
  - **AI substitution:** testing platforms already exist (Hamming, Coval, Cekura, Bluejay; SEARCH-SUMMARY), and Retell is reportedly building QA and testing into its stack (Sacra). Human sign-off for small agencies may have a 12–24-month window (ESTIMATE). Rank accordingly.
  - Never test a production phone line without permission (it costs the end client money).
  - No accuracy guarantee: offer a re-test promise instead.
  - Stay a B2B supplier; the "generic AI agency" is killed.
  - GEO/AI-search white-label is already crowded in 2026 (SEARCH-SUMMARY): do not choose it.

## M5 Wave Desk
*(was M5; absorbs M11 Recovery-for-Share as its "money owed" variant)*

- **What we sell:** fixed-price **operational** readiness for a dated change (a rule, platform policy or programme window), monitoring after the date, and, where money is owed, claim preparation paid only from the money recovered.
- **Who pays:** businesses on a public list of the affected, preferably reached through their middlemen (accountants, associations, platform resellers) who serve many of them.
- **The five filters (a wave must pass all five):**
  - F1: no licence and no legal opinion. We do checklists, records and data, never advice.
  - F2: the affected businesses can be listed and contacted from public sources.
  - F3: the date was confirmed by a primary source in the last 30 days and has not moved twice.
  - F4: there is a repeat stage after the date.
  - F5: the outreach channel is legal for the buyer's country (method.md, Part B, group G).
- **How we find and reach them:** the deadline is the timing trigger. Contact them 6–10 weeks before the date (ESTIMATE), through the middleman where one exists.
- **First step from home (Monday):**
  1. List 10 dated changes due in 3–9 months and score F1–F5.
  2. Pick the best one and confirm its date from the primary source.
  3. Build a one-page readiness checklist and test it on one real affected business's public footprint.
- **Money path:** $200–600 readiness package → $50–150/month monitoring → a middleman (an accountant with 50 affected clients) resells it → $1,000+/month (ESTIMATE). Recovery variant: 10–25% of money recovered (brief's draft range; ESTIMATE).
- **Path steps by design:**
  - Strong: Reply (the deadline), and risk Trust in the recovery variant (paid from money they would not otherwise get).
  - Weak: Read, identity Trust and Repeat. **Flagged weak on Read/Trust/Pay** unless a middleman carries it. Waves die suddenly. Evidence: Indonesia's halal-certification deadline for imported products was moved from 17/18 Oct 2026 to Oct 2028, announced days before it bit (SEARCH-SUMMARY, Emerhub, Tilleke & Gibbins, CIRS; scope differs by source). EUDR moved twice before Reg (EU) 2025/2650.
- **AI and human:** AI reads the rule, maps obligations and drafts records. The human checks every requirement against the primary text and refuses anything that is advice.
- **Hunting grounds (Phase 2):**
  1. *UK illegal-working enforcement in restaurants and takeaways* — Home Office visits rose from 1,675 to 2,195 (+31%) in Jan–Jun 2026 vs 2025, and arrests rose 31% (VERIFIED: gov.uk note updated 7 Aug 2026, plus IBTimes). Restaurants and cafes were fined almost £9m (restaurantonline, May 2026; SEARCH-SUMMARY). Product: right-to-work *record-keeping readiness* via accountants. Not immigration advice, which is regulated by the Immigration Advice Authority (the former OISC, renamed 16 Jan 2025; VERIFIED, multiple).
  2. *UK Gift Aid / Gift Aid Small Donations Scheme claims for small charities and mosques* — about £560–564m of Gift Aid goes unclaimed each year (VERIFIED: HMRC estimate via Charity Tax Group, CAF, Fundraising UK Feb 2026). Annual cycle; paid from recovered money; strong affinity overlap with M8.
  3. *Marketplace, app-store and ad-platform policy deadlines with public dates* — no licence, and sellers are publicly listed.
  4. *EU AI Act transparency duties for chatbots and AI content* — date and scope NOT VERIFIED here; EU cold outreach is limited, so reach agencies or platforms only.
  5. *IEEPA tariff refunds* — only if the Federal Circuit appeal reopens claims, and only as data prep for the importer's licensed broker (licence plane).
- **Planes to watch:** dates move; advice drift (legal, immigration, customs); short life; a wave that needs logins to the client's government portal (prepare files the client uploads themselves).

## M6 Dhaka Ground Desk
*(was M7; the new "Verification Partner" model became its core)*

- **What we sell:** in-country eyes, hands and Bangla for foreign middlemen:
  - (a) primary-source verification: education, employment, address and business existence;
  - (b) supplier and factory existence checks, and checklist-based pre-shipment inspections;
  - (c) "verified in person" checks for remote hires (shared with M2 Mode B).
  The partner's exact checklist work *is* the product.
- **Who pays:** foreign background-screening and verification firms that need Bangladesh coverage; sourcing agents and inspection firms with Bangladesh gaps; small foreign brands sourcing here; foreign employers hiring Bangladeshi remote staff.
- **How we find and reach them:**
  - screening-industry member directories and "international partner network" pages;
  - sourcing-agent directories;
  - posted requests ("need someone in Bangladesh to verify/visit") on Upwork, LinkedIn and Reddit.
  The middleman's vendor-management inbox wants coverage. A coverage gap is the reason to reply.
- **First step from home (Monday):**
  1. Write an honest one-page "Bangladesh verification playbook": 5 check types, the real route, turnaround and fees, each tested on the founders' own documents (e.g., the online certificate-verification service launched in August 2025; SEARCH-SUMMARY).
  2. List 30 foreign screening or sourcing firms that list Bangladesh coverage, or lack it.
  3. Email 10 with one free check offered.
- **Money path:** $15–60 per desk check and $150–300 per site visit or inspection (both ESTIMATE; Phase 2 must find real rates) → 2–3 middlemen sending steady volume → $1,000+/month. Cap breaker: a checklist lets trained helpers in other cities do visits under our sign-off.
- **Path steps by design:**
  - Strong: Home (we are here), identity Trust (physical presence is provable: live video from the site, real faces), Trust by mechanism (these middlemen already use offshore partners), Pay (B2B invoice via Payoneer), Repeat (volume work).
  - Weak: Read (vendor inboxes are cold). **Medium.**
- **Why now:** fake certificates remain widespread in Bangladesh: 471 cases in an April 2026 audit, 1,172 teachers in an earlier audit (SEARCH-SUMMARY, Jagonews, Prothom Alo). Forgers reportedly inserted records into a board database (Daily Star feature, undated; SEARCH-SUMMARY), so database lookups alone are not proof. Remote-hiring fraud rose in 2025–26 (see M2).
- **AI and human:** AI tracks cases, writes Bangla↔English letters and reports, and flags document anomalies. The human calls in Bangla, visits, and signs the finding.
- **Hunting grounds (Phase 2):**
  1. *International background-screening firms* — they need education, employment and address checks in Bangladesh; this is recurring partner work.
  2. *Overseas recruiters, universities and credential evaluators* — they need checks beyond the online database because of forged entries.
  3. *Small foreign apparel, home-textile, jute and leather brands* — supplier existence plus checklist inspections, where a buying house is too big for them.
  4. *Foreign firms hiring Bangladeshi remote staff* — in-person identity proof (with M2 Mode B).
  5. *Foreign SMEs vetting a Bangladeshi counterparty* — does the company, address and director exist?
- **Planes to watch:**
  - Bangladeshi offices open Sunday–Thursday, which clashes with part-time hours.
  - Data protection and consent forms (Bangladesh's personal-data law status UNVERIFIED).
  - Screening firms may demand company registration and E&O insurance.
  - Pressure for informal payments at offices: refuse (halal and legal).
  - Inspection liability: report only, no pass guarantee.
  - Commission from factories is a conflict of interest: take none.
  - Possible BGBA registration for buying agents (UNVERIFIED).

## M7 Platform-Native Seller
*(was M10; absorbs M13 Tool-Wrapped Service, the platform part of M8 Bangla AI Data, and new "asset-led entry")*

- **What we sell:** fixed-scope productized offers and digital assets **inside places where buyers already search and the platform holds trust and money**: Upwork (Project Catalog and proposals), tool partner directories, template and skill stores (the CEO already sells $29 products on Gumroad), AI red-team arenas, and AI-training platforms listing Bengali work.
- **Who pays:** platform buyers. For assets, the buyer of a $29–99 template who then wants it installed ($150–300).
- **How we find and reach them:** platform search and alerts, proposals 1–4 hours after a post (method.md T2), and assets as lead magnets (the buyer self-identifies).
- **First step from home (Monday):**
  1. Pick one offer where demand beats supply: count posts in 30 days against competing listings for the exact offer.
  2. Publish one Catalog project with a real sample.
  3. Spend the month's 10 free Connects on the 2 best-fit posts, 1–4 hours after posting.
- **Money path:** $50–300 first jobs → reviews → repeat buyers at $500–1,500/month. Assets add unit sales, and installs upsell to service. Cap breaker: assets sell without hours; the platform does the selling.
- **Path steps by design:**
  - Strong: Read (platform search), Pay (escrow), Trust after the first reviews.
  - Weak: Impressed and Reply (crowded; Bangladesh is already a large supply country; ESTIMATE), plus a zero-review start.
  - Connects: Upwork Basic gives 10 free per month and extras cost $0.15. The service fee has been variable at 0–15% since 2025-05-01 (SEARCH-SUMMARY, Upwork resource page plus blogs). At a ~$0 budget that means 1–3 proposals a month, so choose carefully.
- **AI and human:** AI delivers and drafts. The human handles QA, buyer messages, and choosing what to bid on.
- **Why now:** Upwork's AI-related gross services volume grew about 22% YoY in Q2 2026, and "AI strategy and consulting" grew 50%+, while total GSV fell about 3.6% (notes §1, SEARCH-SUMMARY from Upwork's Q2 2026 release). Demand is moving to AI-related work, which is where parity exists.
- **Hunting grounds (Phase 2):**
  1. *Upwork Catalog categories for tools under 18 months old* — thin supply, and parity offsets zero reviews.

## Companion critique of draft v1
(Model numbers in this section are the draft-v1 numbers, not the final numbers above.)

### Five problems that run through the whole draft
1. **No capacity math.** Two people at 8 h/week each is about 70 hours a month in total (ESTIMATE: 2 × 8 × 4.3). At an effective $25–40/h that caps founder-delivered revenue near $1,700–2,800/month for the whole company (ESTIMATE). Several drafts promise "$1–5k/month per client". With founder delivery, one such client fills the company. Every final model now carries a capacity line and a lever that breaks the cap: AI doing most of the volume, a middleman doing the selling, or other people doing the delivery under our checks.
2. **"Trust" is treated as one thing. It is three.** (a) Identity: are these people real and who they say? (b) Competence: can they do it? (c) Risk: who loses money if it goes wrong? Different mechanisms fix each: video and real names fix (a); public proof about the buyer's own case fixes (b); pay-after-proof, escrow or fee-from-result fixes (c). A step scored "Trust: medium" hides which one is broken.
3. **The team's two unfair advantages are barely used.** Shared language and identity with 644,881 Bangladeshis in England and Wales at the 2021 census (SEARCH-SUMMARY, Birmingham City Council profile citing the census) and the wider diaspora, and physical presence in Dhaka. Only Shothik and M7 touch them. The partner's strength (exact, checklist-driven work) is never the core product, although inspection, verification and QA are exactly that.
4. **Timezone is ignored.** Dhaka is UTC+6. A UK business's quiet afternoon (15:00 GMT/BST) is 20:00–21:00 in Dhaka. US East Coast office hours (09:00–17:00 EDT) are 19:00–03:00 in Dhaka. Australian office hours are 05:00–13:00 in Dhaka (all ESTIMATE from standard offsets). Live contact favours the UK, the Gulf and Australia. The US works only in its morning (Dhaka evening), which misses restaurants' quiet afternoons (Dhaka 00:00–04:00).
5. **"Free sample" is used everywhere without limits.** Free work for strangers draws people who want free work. The sample must be (a) about the buyer's own case, (b) small enough to make in under 2 hours, (c) incomplete by design, ending in one paid next step with a price on it (ESTIMATE; standard productized-service practice).

### M1 Problem Spotter — KEEP, RESHAPE (absorbs M6 and M12)
- **Wrong:** It sells to the person who suffers the problem, often a single owner who did not ask, may not believe it, and caps at $100–250/month (brief's own lesson).
- **Missing:** *Who owns the fix.* The problem was often made or is maintained by a vendor: the web agency, the ordering platform's reseller, the listings manager, the bot builder. That party has a motive (keep the client) and a budget, and many clients. Also missing: a proof the buyer can check in 30 seconds without trusting us ("call this number", "open this link").
- **Plane:** The "fix" usually needs logins (Google Business Profile manager access, website admin, delivery-app tablet). That breaks design rule 4 at step 2 of the sale. False positives are a second plane: one wrong claim and the stranger is a liar.
- **Fix in v2:** sell the proof to whoever owns the fix; M6 Watchtower becomes M1's "kept fixed" repeat stage; M12 Benchmark becomes M1's scale mode for Read (a public scoreboard people reply to because they are named).

### M2 Replacement Desk — KEEP, RESHAPE
- **Wrong:** A job ad means "we want an employee". The hiring manager is not shopping for a vendor, applications are filtered by an ATS, and many remote ads are "US only" (ESTIMATE). Pitching a vendor into a hiring funnel is a weak Read.
- **Missing:** The free batch "from public material" only works when the task's input and output are both public (listings, content, research, catalogues). Most recurring internal jobs use private data (inbox, CRM, tickets). Also missing: the capacity cap, which this model hits first.
- **Plane:** "Process X units" jobs are exactly what AI tools absorb in 1–2 years, and the killed list already holds most of them (uploads, data entry, bookkeeping).
- **Fix in v2:** reach posters who *want a contractor* (Upwork long-term/hourly posts, contract and "fractional" boards, AI startups hiring human-in-the-loop roles). Add Mode B, which is new: supply a verified Dhaka person we stand behind. It breaks the capacity cap and rides the 2025–26 fake-candidate wave (Gartner: by 2028 one in four candidate profiles worldwide could be fake; SEARCH-SUMMARY, secondary reports only).

### M3 Idea Partner — KEEP, RESHAPE
- **Wrong:** A profit share needs the partner's books. Strangers can under-report profit, and we cannot audit them from Dhaka. That makes Pay a 0 in disguise.
- **Missing:** A revenue line *we can see*: we operate the channel, or the money passes through a tracked link, code or invoice.
- **Plane:** "Then we run it with them" needs their accounts, stock and capital. The CEO's strength is the idea; running a sales channel is a full-time job.
- **Fix in v2:** commission only on revenue visible to us, opened by owners who publicly ask for new revenue (creators seeking sponsors, brands seeking stockists or a new market). It also works as the stage-2 upsell for every other model's clients.

### M4 White-label Bench — KEEP, RESHAPE (absorbs M9)
- **Wrong:** "Do a sample for one of their public clients" can read as poaching. Agencies get white-label pitches daily, and the Indian, Pakistani and Philippine incumbents have portfolios and 24/7 cover.
- **Evidence against the obvious niche:** GEO / AI-search visibility already has 2026 agency rankings, "best GEO agencies" lists and white-label GEO tools from about €49/month (SEARCH-SUMMARY, mostly vendor pages). Its parity window has closed.
- **Missing:** The agency's margin. Agencies typically pay a white-label partner 30–50% of what they charge (ESTIMATE). Payment terms are often net-30 or longer (ESTIMATE).
- **Plane:** Delivery usually needs the end-client's logins, which the agency must grant.
- **Fix in v2:** only categories under ~18 months old where agencies are already selling but cannot yet deliver well, starting with the human sign-off from M9 (AI agent and chatbot QA before go-live and monthly).

### M5 Wave Desk — KEEP, RESHAPE (absorbs M11)
- **Wrong:** The three waves found are poor fits. IEEPA refunds go only to the importer of record, CBP charges no fee, filing is in ACE, a customs-broker licence may be needed, and Phase 3 (Oct 2026) is limited to CIT plaintiffs. Most of that wave is over for newcomers. EUDR has moved before (Reg (EU) 2025/2650 set 2026-12-30) and the buyers are EU operators served by large consultancies; EU cold outreach is mostly off-limits. LDC graduation's buyers are Bangladeshi exporters, which are excluded as final buyers.
- **Missing:** Four filters: (1) no licence and no legal opinion, only operational readiness; (2) the list of affected businesses is public and contactable; (3) the date has not moved twice; (4) a repeat stage after the date.
- **Plane:** Deadlines move, which kills the Reply mechanism overnight. Rules work drifts into legal advice.

### M6 Watchtower — MERGE into M1
- Monitoring software is cheap and everywhere. A "free month on a public target" is free work with no buyer commitment. Its real job is M1's repeat stage ("kept fixed"), not a door-opener.

### M7 Dhaka Ground Desk — KEEP, RESHAPE
- **Wrong:** It assumes factory visits fit 8 h/week. A visit to Gazipur, Savar or Narayanganj is realistically half a day with traffic (ESTIMATE). It takes quality liability for whole shipments. Bangladesh's buying-house industry is mature, and small-MOQ orders are not the country's strength (ESTIMATE).
- **Missing:** Foreign middlemen who need a Bangladesh partner (sourcing agents, inspection firms, background-screening and verification firms) are a better first buyer than small brands. Verification work is checklist work, the partner's strength.
- **New evidence:** Bangladesh launched an online certificate-verification service with an apostille option in August 2025 (SEARCH-SUMMARY), so simple education checks are becoming self-serve. A Daily Star feature reports forgers inserting fake records into a board database (SEARCH-SUMMARY, undated). A database hit alone can therefore confirm a forgery, which keeps human checks at source valuable. Mass fake-certificate findings among teachers continued in 2026 (471 cases in an April 2026 audit; SEARCH-SUMMARY).
- **Plane:** Taking commission from factories breaks buyer trust. A buying-agent registration (BGBA) may be required (unchecked). Travel costs are small but not zero.

### M8 Bangla AI Data Desk — CUT as a standalone; platform route moved to M7
- **Wrong:** Native Bangla is not unique to Bangladesh. India has roughly 97M Bengali speakers (2011 census, ESTIMATE from memory; re-check), and Indian data vendors already operate at scale. The platforms (Mercor, micro1, Invisible, OpenTrain) hire *individuals* at platform-set prices: $14–24/h in one OpenTrain listing, and an "average $38/h" claimed by aigigjobs from its own listings (VENDOR CLAIM). That is a job, not a business.
- **Missing:** Any road to a buyer who pays a *team*. Vendor programmes for AI labs ask for scale and security certifications (ESTIMATE).
- **Kept:** As a cash-flow ground and a parity route inside M7 Platform-Native (platform listings and red-team contests), with the Bengali safety-benchmark papers (arXiv 2608.22335, Aug 2026) as the proof hook.

### M9 Human Sign-off Desk — MERGE into M4
- **Wrong:** An "error guarantee" is open-ended liability for two uninsured people. The end business often does not care until it is burned. Calling a business's AI receptionist ten times costs them money and goodwill.
- **Evidence:** The software side of AI-agent testing is already crowded (Hamming, Coval, Cekura, Bluejay; Retell lists QA partners; SEARCH-SUMMARY). The open gap is the *human* sign-off for small agencies that deploy bots for SMB clients and cannot afford enterprise testing tools (ESTIMATE, to test in Phase 2).
- **Fix:** Sell to the deployer (agency or bot vendor), with a capped re-test promise instead of a guarantee.

### M10 Platform-Native Seller — KEEP
- **Wrong:** "Pick an under-supplied category" ignores that Bangladesh is already a large Upwork supply country and that new profiles start with zero reviews. Connects cost money: 10 free a month on the Basic plan, $0.15 each after that, and the service fee is variable at 0–15% since 2025-05-01 (SEARCH-SUMMARY, Upwork resource page plus blogs). With a ~$0 budget that is about two proposals a month.
- **Missing:** Platforms other than Upwork where trust and payment are held by the platform: tool partner directories, template stores, contests and bounties.
- **Fix:** Absorb M13 (asset-led entry) and the platform part of M8.

### M11 Recovery-for-Share — CUT as standalone; mechanism kept inside M5 and as a pricing lever
- Most recovery niches are already software-automated (marketplace reimbursements, parcel refunds) or licensed (customs, some US unclaimed-property finders, UK claims management) (ESTIMATE; Phase 2 must verify per niche). Recovery needs the client's data or logins, and the collection risk is high: the client receives the refund, then must pay us. "Fee only from money recovered" stays as a risk-reversal tool in method.md.

### M12 Benchmark Publisher — MERGE into M1 as its Read engine
- Authority takes months. Naming low scorers carries defamation and fairness risk (in the UK a business must show serious financial loss to sue, but the risk still exists; ESTIMATE). Journalists ignore unknown publishers without a hook. Its strength (people reply when they are named) is real, so it becomes M1's scale mode and a playbook tactic.

### M13 Tool-Wrapped Service — CUT as standalone
- Distribution is the plane (the CEO's itch.io lesson), and a tool bug costs the client money while the team cannot debug it. It stays only as the late scale stage of a proven service, and as "asset-led entry" in M7.
