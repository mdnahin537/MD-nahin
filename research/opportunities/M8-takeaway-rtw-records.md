# M8 — Right-to-work records readiness for UK Bangladeshi takeaways, via their accountants
**Model:** M8 Affinity Desk (M5 wave mechanism) · **Companion:** Phase 2 Opus, 2026-10-29
**One line:** From **1 October 2026**, UK right-to-work checks extend beyond employees to workers, individual subcontractors and gig or platform labour, such as a takeaway's self-employed delivery drivers. Fines are **£45,000 per worker for a first breach and £60,000 for a repeat**. We sell a records-readiness pack: an anonymised file-index audit, a checklist for the new categories, and a follow-up-check calendar. We sell it to the Bangladeshi accountants and payroll bureaus who serve takeaways, never as immigration advice. Delivered in Bangla and English.

## (h) Red team
**Strongest reason it fails:** the owners with the real exposure won't let strangers near their staff files, and compliant owners don't feel the pain. Accountants won't risk their name on an unknown Dhaka supplier in an area where one mistake costs a client £45,000.
- Law firms (£750–2,000), HR platforms and digital ID providers already serve the buyers who pay.
- Trust here is the reverse of Gift Aid: we can't show a public gap; we can only warn.

**Counter-evidence searches I ran:**
1. "right to work audit price … small business UK": found law-firm pricing only (FSP £750/£2,000) and no budget tier for takeaways. That is a gap, or a sign that small takeaways don't buy audits.
2. Home Office penalty register Jan–Mar 2026: South-Asian takeaways are prominent (≥ 20 of 291 entries; E3). The risk is real, but this list is of non-payers and repeat offenders: buyers in crisis, not prospects.
3. IAA guidance on employer-facing advice: **NOT FOUND** (the search returned 2012–2021 OISC documents only). The G2 boundary rests on the statutory definition.
