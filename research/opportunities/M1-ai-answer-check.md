# M1 — AI Answer Fact-Check: what ChatGPT, Gemini and Google's AI tell customers about a business, checked against the truth and fixed at the source
**Model:** M1 Proof-First Fixer · **Companion:** Phase 2 Opus (M1 owner), 2026-10-30
**Hunting grounds used:** #4 (public AI that gives wrong facts) and #5 (benchmarks as the Read engine). Not restaurant-only: clinics, trades, gyms and hotels have the same problem.
**One line:** Each month, ask the main AI assistants 10 fixed factual questions per client location (open today? holiday hours? phone? price range? booking link? "do you take X insurance?"), record the answers with timestamps, compare them with a client-confirmed fact sheet, trace each wrong answer to its source (Yelp, Bing, Apple, an old page on the website, a directory), fix the source, and re-test. Sold white-label to local-marketing agencies that now sell "AI visibility", and to multi-location brands.
**Labels:** VERIFIED · E3 · VENDOR CLAIM · SEARCH-SUMMARY · ESTIMATE · NOT FOUND. Pages opened 2026-10-30.
**Does it beat the two required opportunities?** **No.** It has the best demand trend in M1 but the worst substitution risk and a reproducibility hole in its proof. Its value is as a line inside the bundle (see M1-summary).

## (c) Filters
- **Work level:** medium (question design, truth sheet, source tracing, fixes across Yelp/Bing/Apple/website).
- **Demand trend:** 2024: ChatGPT local sources = websites 58% (BrightLocal); 2025 → 2026: AI for local recommendations 6% → 45% (VERIFIED); 2026-07: 48–56% full-match accuracy (Insites). Strongly rising.
- **AI substitution (12–24 months): very high.** Checking AI answers against a known fact sheet is software's home ground; Yext already feeds OpenAI directly. The human residue is "what is actually true" and fixing third-party sources, which is listings work.
- **Team fit:** good (checklist, screenshots); no live calls.
- **Halal:** filter licensed venues; clinics, trades and gyms are mostly clean (check each).

## (h) Red team
**Strongest reason it fails:** the proof is not self-checkable (AI answers change between runs and users), and the measurement is being given away free by vendors (Insites free audit; £9.99 deep audits; BrightLocal from $31). We would be selling a free thing to buyers who are already being pitched it.
**Counter-evidence search run:** "AI visibility audit service local business price" (2026-10-30) found a crowded price ladder from free/£9.99 to £280/month; no posted need from agencies for outside fact-checkers (NOT FOUND).

## (i) vs Shothik (Read, Trust, Pay)
- **Read:** same (1): business inbox, but the topic is saturated by free audits.
- **Trust:** better (2/2/2 via the agency's access), but Impressed is **worse** than Shothik (1 vs 2): Shothik's "Google says X, site says Y" is stable; an AI answer isn't.
- **Pay:** better (2, B2B).

## (j) Intelligence verdict
The trend is the strongest in M1 and the formula even passes two-key, but the formula misses two things: a proof that changes when the buyer re-checks it, and a measurement layer that software is giving away. What's left for humans, establishing the truth and fixing Yelp, Bing, Apple and the website, *is* listings work. So this isn't a separate business; it's the best **reason** to buy the listings line in 2026 ("AI repeats your wrong hours"). Use it as a line in the bundle's letter, never as a product on its own.
