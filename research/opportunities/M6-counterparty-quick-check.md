# M6 — Bangladesh Counterparty Quick-Check: desk "does this company exist and is it who it says?" reports for foreign buyers, sold through due-diligence and business-information firms
**Model:** M6 Dhaka Ground Desk · **Companion:** Phase 2 Opus, 2026-10-29 · **Helpers:** none for this file
**One line:** A fixed-scope desk report on one Bangladeshi company (supplier, distributor, buyer or partner) in 5 working days: registry status (RJSC), trade licence, TIN/BIN, export-registration and association membership, a Bangla phone check with the company and two trade references, address check on public maps, Bangla media and court-news scan. **No site visit** in the base product; a visit is a rare paid add-on. Sold first to foreign due-diligence and business-information firms that resell "freshly investigated" reports, and second to foreign SMEs directly.
**Labels:** VERIFIED (I opened the primary page on 2026-10-29) · PRESS (news article opened) · VENDOR CLAIM · SEARCH-SUMMARY (page not opened) · ESTIMATE · NOT FOUND.

**Why this road exists (the price gap):**
- The US Commercial Service's own Bangladesh counterparty report, the **International Company Profile, now costs $1,050 (partial) or $2,000 (full) per market** (VERIFIED, trade.gov user-fee page, opened 2026-10-29). Older schedules showed **$150 (partial) / $700 (full) for small businesses** (SEARCH-SUMMARY, trade.gov snippet and a sema.org copy "as of 3/20"). Blogs date the removal of small-business discounts to **22 Jul 2026** (SEARCH-SUMMARY).
- The best-known Dhaka vendor, Station Dhaka, lists **Quick Verify $800 per entity** (RJSC, trade licence, TIN, ownership, red flags, 5–7 days), Standard $2,000, Comprehensive $4,500 (with site visit), factory audit add-on $1,500, monitoring $500/quarter (VERIFIED as VENDOR CLAIM, stationdhaka.com/services/due-diligence.html).
- So a desk check at **$150–300** sits far below both, and is plausible as a wholesale input for foreign report vendors.

## (e) Money path and 3-scenario estimate
**Path:** one paid sample report to a DD/business-information firm ($150) → wholesale price $150–250 per report → 2–3 resellers sending 3–8 reports a month each → $1,000+/month. A "monitoring" add-on ($50/quarter per company) adds slow repeat.
Formula: reachable resellers × positive-reply rate × conversion × reports/month × price.

## (h) Red team
**Strongest reason it fails:** foreign SMEs rarely buy counterparty reports at all, and the resellers that do already have a Bangladesh source (D&B's local affiliate, Station Dhaka, global investigator networks). A new two-person desk has no Read route and no track record, and the price is low because the report is low-value without a visit.
**Counter-evidence searches I ran:** (1) "Bangladesh company credit report price": no published reseller price found; D&B works through a local affiliate (SEARCH-SUMMARY); Creditsafe partnered with Interlinkages for Bangladesh (SEARCH-SUMMARY, BIIA). (2) "due diligence firm in-country researcher Bangladesh": no recruitment page found; only freelance directories (Paydesk, journalist.net) and a Fiverr seller offering "company and director research" from Bangladesh (SEARCH-SUMMARY). So the supply side is crowded and the buyer-side recruitment is invisible.

## (j) Intelligence verdict
The price gap is real and VERIFIED: $1,050–2,000 (US government) and $800 (Dhaka vendor) against a $150–300 desk check. But a price gap is not a road. The buyer who would pay is either an SME we cannot find or a reseller who already has a Bangladesh source. **I would not lead with this.** Keep it as a second product for any screening or verification client won through the bet (M6-institution-liaison), where the same desk skills apply and trust already exists.
