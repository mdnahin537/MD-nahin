# M2 — "Met in person in Dhaka": verified hires for small firms that just posted a remote-worldwide role
**Model:** M2 Replacement Desk (Mode B, sold directly to the end employer) · **Companion:** Phase 2 Opus, 2026-10-29
**One line:** When a small firm posts a remote role open to Bangladesh or "worldwide", we email the founder within days with two Dhaka candidates. We have met both in person, checked their ID and degrees against originals and at source, and filmed each doing a task like the posted one. One-time fee after 30 days of work, free replacement within 90 days.

**Why this file exists:** it is the model's own thesis (Mode B rides the fake-candidate wave). I tested it head-on. **Result: the wave is real, but the buyer's answer to it is to distrust unknown offshore agencies, not to hire one.**

In-person rounds came back at Google, Cisco and McKinsey in Aug 2025 (VERIFIED: the OECD.AI incident record lists WSJ and Axios, both 2025-08-12). **That trend cuts both ways:** it raises the value of in-person proof and pushes firms toward local hires.
- **AI-substitution risk:** low for the in-person check; the placed person's own role may be automated (screen roles with G4 in mind).
- **Team fit:** strong. The CEO handles judgment and calls and the partner runs the checklist. One Dhaka meeting per candidate is allowed by the CEO's answers.
- **Halal:** screen gambling, alcohol and interest-lending posters; no fees from candidates.

## (h) Red team
**Strongest reason it fails:** buyers frightened by fake candidates trust *their own* process (in-person rounds, background-check vendors such as Checkr: 62% of firms now use third-party checks, up from 53%, VERIFIED) more than an unknown offshore agency, and DOJ's 2025 case shows fraud rings dressed up as agencies. Meanwhile Bangladesh's established IT firms already supply office-based developers, so we have no edge in tech roles.
**Counter-evidence search I ran:** "companies reinstate in-person interviews 2025 2026 … remote hiring restricted" → OECD.AI record (2025-08-12, VERIFIED) and secondary write-ups: firms move final rounds in person, which disadvantages every offshore candidate unless we offer an in-person proxy. "fake job candidates remote hiring 2026 survey" → Checkr, Pin, Greenhouse figures; Pin says 22.4% of recruiters confirmed a deepfake or identity swap (SEARCH-SUMMARY). The fear is real; the evidence that buyers *pay an outside verifier abroad* is NOT FOUND.

## (i) vs Shothik
- **Read: same.** Shothik: a stranger's Facebook request; here: a stranger's email to a founder. Both are cold, and both have a trigger.
- **Trust: mixed.** Identity is better (in-person verification is the product), but "unknown agency" adds a fraud smell that Shothik doesn't have. Competence: worse (no placements yet).
- **Pay: better.** A B2B card or ACH fee vs a restaurant's Zelle/cash, but collection risk is higher (they can hire around us).

## (j) Intelligence verdict
The formula would score this better than it deserves, because Road, Letter and Reply look like 2s: thousands of posts, each with a trigger. The truth is in Trust: **in a fraud wave, an unknown offshore agency is a red flag, not a remedy.** I would not lead with this. It becomes viable only after the team has 3–5 placements through an intermediary or Upwork and can show named, checkable references. Then the same email works far better.
