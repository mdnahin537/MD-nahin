## 0. Bottom line (for the manager)
1. **Forced waves pay only the early.** The cleanest wave of 2026, the EU withdrawal button (in force 2026-06-19), made one app 2,320 reviews in 10 months. It launched 2026-01-09, five months early. Apps launched two weeks before or after the deadline have 1–5 reviews (VERIFIED app pages). The next display-type rule, the EU guarantee notice of 2026-09-27, drew ≥11 apps in six weeks; the best has 14 reviews (VERIFIED). **The widgets get cloned by AI within weeks; only timing and trust win.**
2. **Shopify eats generic compliance and AI-commerce tools.** Spring '26 shipped native "Product compliance disclosure" (warnings on products), so GPSR apps launched in 2026 sit at 0 reviews. Every Shopify store now serves /llms.txt, /agents.md and /.well-known/ucp by default, and 8 "AI visibility" apps together earned about 1 review in 90 days. Agentic selling in ChatGPT, Copilot, Perplexity, Google AI Mode and Gemini is native, as are duties via Managed Markets (all VERIFIED). → **AVOID these as app niches.**
3. **The best wave left in the 6-month window is platform-forced, not legal.** Script tags stop running on storefronts on **2027-03-01**, and creating them has been blocked since 2026-10-01 (VERIFIED shopify.dev). Non-expiring app tokens fail on **2027-01-01** (VERIFIED). 14 of 21 sampled Shopify brand stores still load script tags today (VERIFIED raw pages). One developer says 70–75% of its users are still on script tags, and Shopify says there is "no programmatic way" to switch them (developer posts in the official thread, 2026-08/10). The work left is human: click-by-click embed activation, audits and app replacement for thousands of non-technical merchants. **That is a Dhaka-shaped job with a hard date: a service sprint, sold to app developers and merchants, Nov 2026–Mar 2027.**
4. Waves that are real but **too early** (6–20 months out): UK subscription regime (January 2027, VERIFIED gov.uk), Australia's unfair-trading/drip-pricing/subscription ban (2027-07-01, VERIFIED treasury.gov.au), the UK "fake was-price" consultation (autumn 2026, VERIFIED), legacy customer-accounts sunset (date still unannounced on 2026-10-30, VERIFIED), EUDR for micro/small firms (2027-06-30, VERIFIED), and UK £135 relief removal (by Oct 2028, VERIFIED). ## 3. What the store checks teach (the CEO's "demand first" rule, tested on forced waves)
- **Timing beats product.** Withdrawal-button apps by launch date vs deadline (2026-06-19): −161 days → 2,320 reviews; −100 → 578; −43 → 329; −14 → 5; +25 → 4; +31 → 1 (VERIFIED app pages). WordPress shows the same curve: the 6,000-install plugin was added 2026-03-26; most after June sit at 0–500 (VERIFIED api.wordpress.org).
- **Velocity after the deadline is small but not zero.** The leader still gets ~59 reviews per 90 days and Revoq ~57 (ESTIMATE from newest-review pages). Late entrants get almost nothing because merchants install whatever ranks first.
- **Compliance widgets are the easiest thing for AI coders to clone.** GARAN: ≥11 listings in 6 weeks. GPSR: 13 sampled, all 0 reviews (VERIFIED). This is exactly the "coders with AI" threat the CEO named. In these niches it is already real.
- **Platform-forced waves differ in kind.** The fix needs a human click inside each merchant's store: turning on an app embed. Shopify gave developers no API for it (Shopify reply in the official thread, 2026-08-25). An AI clone can't do that from a listing. A fast human desk can.
- **Round 2's "compliance has little store demand" needs a correction.** It holds for narrow B2B compliance (Peppol, ZATCA). It fails for **broad B2C display rules with a hard date in a lawsuit-prone market** (Germany): those produced the fastest review growth seen this round, but only for apps early by 3–5 months.
