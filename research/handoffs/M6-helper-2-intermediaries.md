## Answers

### A. Do any publish that they pay fees to Bangladeshi institutions, or use in-country agents?
- Pay fees: DU's own release (Daily Star, link above): intermediaries pay USD 50 per student. VERIFIED, but it is DU's statement, not the intermediaries'. QC states a per-institution "surcharge in case a university charges a fee to third parties" (https://qualificationcheck.com/organisation-faqs/). DataFlow states some issuing authorities charge a release fee payable "directly with the issuing authority" (https://dataflowgroup.com/faqs-dataflow-verification/). TEC sells DVS "at an additional cost" (https://evalcompany.com/document-verification-services/).
- In-country agents: QC says "our international teams form part of that process" where local capability is needed (blog link in row 1); a Teesside document mentions a QC South Asia regional office (SEARCH-SUMMARY). Ecctis lists an "Associates Network" (purpose unchecked). No intermediary names a Bangladesh agent. Per helper 1, InfoMart and others use "in-country partners" for screening.

### B. Bangladesh-specific rules
- DU: USD 50, Syndicate decision 22 Nov 2015; Controller of Examinations verifies, registrar emails the report (Daily Star, 6 Apr 2026).
- UHR: HSC proof must come from the Board, "not the school" (UHR Bangladesh page).
- IERF: "PHOTOCOPIES of official academic records"; rejects college-issued records (search preview, page old).
- ERES: SSC/HSC accepted as "Electronic Record (Institution-Verified)" or attested copy (search preview).
- Vanderbilt (a university, not an intermediary): Bangladeshi records stamped by the Controller of Examination, sealed envelope (search preview).
- QC: Bangladesh is a rising-risk origin, 9.5% fraud-signal rate (July 2026 report).
- No intermediary publishes a Bangladesh surcharge or longer turnaround on its own page, except that Zinc/CRS/others in helper 1 show Bangladesh turnaround.

### C. Counter-evidence (Bangladeshi institutions answering online and fast)
- BUP (Bangladesh University of Professionals) built an online certificate-verification portal with BdREN, agreement Sep 2024: https://bdren.net.bd/news/175 (VERIFIED). Sonargaon University and SUST run own portals; Dhaka Tribune reports a Secondary and Higher Education Division "One-Stop Service" via mygov.bd (SEARCH-SUMMARY; Dhaka Tribune returned 403).
- Against that: Prothom Alo op-ed says most institutions lack online verification (SEARCH-SUMMARY). No national portal for foreign intermediaries found. DU itself works by email plus a USD 50 fee, so DU is reachable if the fee arrives.
- WES-QC page claims most verifications complete in 5-6 working days overall, not Bangladesh-specific, so fast answers exist at least for some institutions.

## What contradicts or complicates the idea
- DU says the process works when the intermediary pays the USD 50. The only documented failure is a buyer (UHR / the Swedish authority) refusing to pay. A desk that fronts the fee must find a buyer willing to reimburse the buyer's own cost - exactly what UHR refused.
- QC is the strongest fit but already runs a global operation with "international teams" and a fraud-detection product; it may build local capacity itself rather than buy.
- Most credential evaluators (WES, ECE, Educational Perspectives, TEC, IERF, JS&A) mainly want records sent by the issuing institution to them, which the student arranges; a desk would be paid by the student, not the evaluator.
- AMS India identity is unconfirmed; do not email AMS Inform assuming it is DU's sender.
- Eight rows have no email at all; outreach for those goes through forms.
