## What this means (5 lines)
1. Cheapest automatic fix for hours: free if the restaurant's POS pushes it (Square -> Google per Square's help; DoorDash/Clover take hours from the POS) or if the owner sets Special hours on Google, which itself suggests holiday dates. Toast does NOT push hours to Google (Toast's own help says edit Google directly). Cheapest paid push: Moz Local Lite $16-20/location/mo (engine coverage not confirmed by me), BrightLocal Manage $40/mo annual ($485/yr), Marqii $90/mo (Toast hours sync, restaurant-only).
2. Cheapest HUMAN review response I could verify: The Social Station $49/month per hotel or F&B outlet (promo, 3 months free first); Marqii managed AI+human $100/mo for up to 50 reviews ($2.00 each) falling to $0.75 each at 500; freelancers Fiverr $55/mo and ComeUp $95/45 replies (search previews only). Agency-grade: The HOTH $275/location wholesale, Widewail $750/rooftop.
3. Do agencies buy white-label review responses? Yes by vendor claim only: The HOTH sells managed responses at $275/location wholesale and says agencies resell at $500-$900/mo. White-label software also sells to agencies (Synup Premium+, BrightLocal, Vendasta $99-$999/mo minimums). I found no independent buyer evidence.
4. Software does not kill the review idea: AI replies cost $0-$80/location (ReplyOnTheFly $9.99, Moz Reviews AI +$10, Reputation.com $80) but BrightLocal 2026 says 50% of consumers are put off by templated replies, and Google's own AI suggested replies are still a limited test (US/Brazil/India, as of July 2026).
5. Listings software exists at $16-$90/location but Yext (Listings) says small businesses should come through resellers and gives no public price, and Uberall/Birdeye hide prices, so the listings gap is price transparency and done-for-you labour, not missing tools. ~55% of price cells are VERIFIED on the vendor's own page (about 61% if own-page VENDOR CLAIMs count).
